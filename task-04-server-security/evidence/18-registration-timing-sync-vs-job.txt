##### Reading of the numbers (written by Claude, from the output above)
  Queued registration, requests 2 to 8: 0.37 to 0.49 s, median about 0.46 s. That time is mostly the Argon2id hash (about 77 ms, docs/hashing.md) and the call to the breach service (docs/breach-check.md).
  Request 1 took 2.69 s because it was the first in a freshly started server: it also started the queue connection and created the queue. This is a one-off cost per server start, and it is in the queued figure, not hidden.
  Handing one email to Gmail took 2.1 to 2.5 s in this run.
  So a registration that sent the email INSIDE the request would take about 0.46 + 2.3 = roughly 2.8 s, about six times the queued 0.46 s. This sum is an estimate from two separate measurements. I did not build and time a synchronous version of the route.
  The bigger problem is not the average. Earlier today the same connection to Gmail sometimes hung for 30 s with "Greeting never received" (evidence/16, 16a, 17a try 1). A synchronous registration would have hung for 30 s on those occasions, and the patient would see a spinning button and no confirmation. With the queue, the patient gets the answer in under half a second and the worker retries the email in the background.
