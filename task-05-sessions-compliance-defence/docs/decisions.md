# Task 5 decisions

Recorded 4 November 2026. **Drafted by Claude.** Claude proposed each choice with its reason, and I accepted them all ("accept all"). The reasons below come from CarePoint's own situation (a private clinic in Lagos, patients on low-cost Android phones with slow or unstable data, and one front-desk admin on a shared computer). Each number stays mine only if I can defend its reason in a viva, so I need to read each one and change any I do not believe.

| # | Decision | Choice | Reason from my business | Accepted consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `SameSite` | **Lax** | Patients will open CarePoint from links in emails and WhatsApp messages. Strict would log them out on each such click. Lax still stops cross-site `POST`s, and everything that changes data is a `POST`. I will also check the `Origin` header on those routes. | A link on another site can make the browser send the cookie on a plain page visit, so no `GET` may ever change data. |
| 2 | Session length | **7 days for patients, 8 hours for the admin** | Patients book rarely on their own phones and do not want to log in each time. The front desk sees every patient and uses a shared computer, so a stolen cookie must be worth less: one working day. | A stolen patient cookie works for up to 7 days unless the patient logs out, resets the password or requests deletion. |
| 3 | Gating | **Soft** shipped, **hard** built on a branch | A first-time patient on a slow phone should see doctors and services before meeting a wall. The site asks for an account only when confirming a booking, and keeps what was typed. | Some people will type a booking and then not sign up. |
| 4 | Login before the email is verified | **Allowed, but booking is blocked until verified** | My mail lands in Spam and the worker is started by hand. Blocking login would lock out honest patients whenever either fails. | Someone can register with another person's email and log in as an unverified account. It can neither book nor see anything, and the real owner gets the "someone tried to register" notice. |
| 5 | Deletion grace period | **14 days**, purge job **once a day at night** | A patient who asks by accident can cancel. Pending bookings and payments have time to settle. Still prompt enough for "without undue delay". | Personal data stays, in anonymised form, for up to 14 days after the request. |
| 6 | Admin-only action | **Record a payment as paid** | It is the front desk's real job, and it uses what Task 4 built: `Payment`, an append-only `PaymentEvent`, and "recorded by". A patient who opens the admin address gets a 403. | Payment rows are kept after a deletion request (conflict to write up in `retention-matrix.md`). The legal retention period must be checked by me, not assumed. |
| 7 | Who checks the session | **`src/proxy.ts` checks only that a cookie exists. `getCurrentUser()` in `src/lib/currentUser.ts` asks the database.** Every page or route that shows or changes data calls the helper itself. | The Next.js guide for this version says the proxy runs on every route, including prefetches, so it should not query the database, and that the real check belongs next to the data. A made-up cookie passes the proxy and is stopped by the page (`evidence/06a`). | **Different from the brief**, which describes one middleware doing the whole check. A page that forgets to call the helper is unprotected. The proxy is a convenience, not the lock. |
| 8 | How soft gating works | **`/book` stays public. The server decides at "Book".** A logged-out visitor's choices (doctor and time, nothing personal) are kept in that browser tab, and login sends them back with `?next=/book`. The booking form no longer asks for a name or phone: they come from the account. An unverified patient verifies on the account page. | Same reason as decision 3, plus: a patient with a verified account should not type their name twice, and an unverified one needs some way to verify, because the code screen used to exist only inside sign-up. | `?next=` is restricted to `/book` and `/account` so it cannot send anyone to another site. A person who closes the tab loses their kept choices. The hard version is built on the branch `hard-gating`. |
| 9 | The admin account and "record a payment as paid" | **An admin exists only if I run `npm run create-admin`** (details from `.env`, then I remove the password). The website can never make one: registering with `role: admin` makes a patient. **The role is checked on the server for the page and for the API**, and a patient gets a real HTTP 403 (this uses Next.js's experimental `forbidden()`, switched on in `next.config.ts`). Staff type the amount in naira at the desk: **I did not invent a price list**, so nothing fills the amount in. The booking row is locked while a payment is recorded. | Recording a payment is the front desk's job, and it is the one action where a patient pretending to be staff would cost the clinic money. Locking stops two clicks, or two staff, from recording one visit twice. | The page and the API trust the session's role, and nothing else. `forbidden()` is experimental and could change in a later Next.js. Staff can type a wrong amount, and nothing yet lets them correct it (a new event would be needed). |
| 10 | How account deletion works | **A patient asks, with their password. The account becomes `pending_deletion` and every session ends. For 14 days they can log in and cancel, but not book. A nightly job (02:00 Lagos, a pg-boss schedule) then ANONYMISES the account** (placeholder name and email, phone erased, no password, login impossible) **in one transaction. The booking and payment records are kept.** Staff accounts are never purged. | Decision 5 gave 14 days and a nightly run. Anonymising, not deleting the row, is what lets payment records stay (decision 6): payments point to bookings, and bookings to the patient. Asking for the password stops a stolen session or an unlocked phone from starting a deletion. | **Booking and payment records stay for ever**: I have not decided a retention period, and the legal one must be checked by me (`retention-matrix.md`). The wording shown to patients on the account page is a draft, and `privacy.md` must agree with it. Nobody is emailed about a request, a cancellation or a purge. |

The Task 5 checklist says "node-cron **or** a pg-boss schedule". My own Task 4 code already runs a pg-boss worker. I chose pg-boss. The node-cron line in `AGENT-RULES.md` therefore no longer matches what I build, and I should update it.

Claude's suggestion is the pg-boss schedule: it is already installed, it keeps its state in PostgreSQL so a restart does not lose the schedule, and it needs no second scheduler. The cost is that the purge only runs while the worker is running (`npm run worker`).
